About the Project

Is AI really replacing human work? Our project examines how public expectations compare with what AI systems can actually do and what is changing in the workplace.

Through data visualizations, we explore public perceptions, measured AI capabilities, and patterns of AI use and employment. We aim to highlight what the evidence shows and what remains uncertain.

Data Sources

Our project draws on and evaluates datasets from the following research and statistical sources:

  • Stanford AI Index 2026 — public opinion, technical performance, and economic indicators.
  • Pew Research Center — public and AI-expert expectations about jobs.
  • Ipsos AI Monitor — views about AI across countries.
  • Anthropic Economic Index — observed patterns of AI use in occupational tasks.
  • Eurostat Labour Force Survey — historical European employment by occupation.
  • U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) — occupational employment and wages and employment projections.

What do the planning domain names mean?
  • Blocksworld: Reorder stacks of blocks under movement rules.
  • Childsnack: Prepare and serve meals while satisfying constraints.
  • Floortile: Coordinate robots to paint floor tiles.
  • Miconic: Plan elevator pickups and passenger deliveries.
  • Rovers: Plan exploration, sampling, and data transmission.
  • Sokoban: Push boxes to targets without blocking the path.
  • Spanner: Collect tools and tighten nuts in the required order.
  • Transport: Move packages between locations using vehicles.

Each domain has 45 benchmark tasks per condition. LAMA is a classical planning algorithm, not a language model. Sources: Stanford AI Index 2026, Figures 2.4.9–2.4.10; original planning study.
